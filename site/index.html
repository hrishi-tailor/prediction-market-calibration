Are prediction markets calibrated?

When Kalshi or Polymarket prices an outcome at 70¢, does it happen 70% of the time? I priced 46,168 resolved markets at four moments before they closed, and checked what actually happened.

by hrishi tailor code methodology dataset

the short answer

  1. 01

    Mostly, yes. Across every price, outcomes land within one to two points of what the market implied, on both platforms.

  2. 02

    But Kalshi overprices longshots. The bias grows as a market nears its close and lives in thinly traded markets. Polymarket shows none.

  3. 03

    And you can't profit from it. Buying Kalshi contracts priced under 2¢ returned −89% on average after fees.

1What "calibrated" means

A price is a forecast. If you bought every contract priced at 70¢, a calibrated market would pay out on 70% of them, no more and no less. Calibration says nothing about whether a market is smart, only whether its numbers can be read as probabilities.

Final prices can't test this: by the time a market closes, the outcome is usually known and the price has already moved to 0¢ or 100¢. So I priced each market 30 days, 7 days, 1 day and 1 hour before it closed, using only data from before that moment.

On Kalshi a day before close, contracts priced at 0.7¢ paid out 0.2% of the time.

2The longshot bias

One number summarises calibration: the slope b of a logistic regression of outcomes on prices. A slope of 1 is perfect. Above 1, cheap contracts are overpriced and expensive ones underpriced, the favourite–longshot bias long documented in horse-race betting. Below 1, prices are overconfident.

On Kalshi the slope climbs steadily as markets approach their close. Polymarket stays close to 1 throughout, dipping slightly below it in the final day.

3Where the bias lives

Split by volume, the picture sharpens. On Kalshi the least-traded third of markets carries the bias and the most-traded third is calibrated. Polymarket's busiest markets lean the other way, toward overconfidence, with weather markets the most overconfident of all.

4Can you make money from it?

No. Trading at the quoted price on Kalshi a day before close, buying Yes at the ask or No at one minus the bid and paying the taker fee, cheap contracts lose heavily whichever side they're on. Favourites lose only the spread and fee. No price bucket earns a significantly positive return.

5Three ways this could have been wrong

Each of these can fake a favourite–longshot bias or a calibration error, so each was checked explicitly.

1.08

Tick size

Whole-cent quotes compress extreme prices toward 50¢. Refitting on 5–95¢ prices only, Kalshi's one-day slope is still 1.08 (95% CI 1.02–1.14).

10%

Stopping time

"Will X happen by D?" markets close when X happens, so their close time depends on the outcome. That share of Polymarket markets closed early; without them its one-day slope is 0.93, so the conclusion holds.

0.21¢

Cost of capital

Money locked in a contract also pulls prices toward 50¢, but at 5% a year by at most that much over 30 days, far too little to explain the bias.

6How it was built

  • Data. Public Kalshi and Polymarket APIs, fetched through a rate-limited, resumable Python pipeline. Kalshi prices are bid/ask midpoints from hourly candles, with spreads over 10¢ dropped; Polymarket prices are hourly for the two weeks before close.
  • Sampling. At most 10 markets per series or event, so a few hourly weather and index series can't drown out everything else. All sampling is seeded.
  • Uncertainty. Markets in one event resolve together, so every interval treats the event, not the market, as the unit: a cluster bootstrap for the buckets, cluster-robust standard errors for the regressions.
  • Engineering. A tested Python package with CI, and a released dataset that reproduces every figure here in under a minute.

limitations

  • Lifetime volume is measured after the fact, so "high volume" means markets that ended up liquid, not liquidity at the moment of the price.
  • Kalshi doesn't expose a scheduled close separate from the actual one, so the stopping-time check covers Polymarket only.
  • The two platforms list very different markets, so their overall accuracy isn't directly comparable.
